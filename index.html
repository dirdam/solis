Earth from the Sun's point of view

Earth's own reference frame

Earth is always shown here from Earth's own reference frame: the map is oriented so that Earth's geographical north pole points to the top, exactly as in a standard world map.

The Sun's reference frame

This project instead asks what a map of Earth would look like from the Sun's reference frame. Picture "solino" citizens living on the Sun: to draw a map, they would orient it using the Sun's own geographical north and the plane of the solar system, not Earth's geographical north. Because Earth's rotation axis is tilted relative to that plane, the resulting projection is rotated compared to the familiar Earth-centered map. Use the slider below to explore how the map changes as Earth spins through the hours of the day.

How it works

This app renders Earth from the Sun's perspective at a given month and hour by:

  • Mapping the equirectangular projection onto a 3D sphere
  • Tilting it by Earth's fixed axial tilt (~23.5°) relative to the solar system's plane
  • Spinning it around its polar axis to match the selected hour of the day
  • Reprojecting back to an equirectangular view
  • Axial tilt: fixed at Earth's real ~23.5° tilt — this never changes with the seasons, only Earth's orientation relative to the Sun does
  • Month of the year: sets how directly the Sun's light falls on each hemisphere, from a full tilt toward the Sun at the solstices to none at the equinoxes — shown as the Sun's-eye view's Earth tilt, the day/night terminator and Sun marker in Earth's own view, and the lit side of the 3D globe
  • Hour of the day: spins the Earth so the meridian facing the Sun matches the selected time
  • 3D view: the same tilt and lighting rendered as an actual sphere you can drag to look around — the camera stays level with the orbital plane, so the axial tilt always reads as a lean rather than a bird's-eye view
